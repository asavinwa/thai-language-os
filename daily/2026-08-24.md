# 24 August 2026

## What I noticed

A lot of the Thai I hear in songs, Netflix and conversations with family is still only partly understandable.

I often recognise individual words or pieces of a sentence, but not enough of the whole thought to respond naturally. That means speaking practice alone is not the main bottleneck right now: **real-time comprehension is**.

This is an important progress marker rather than a failure. I know enough Thai to notice what I am missing. The next stage is making natural spoken Thai feel less like a stream of partially recognised words.

## Current learning priority

For the next few weeks, shift the emphasis toward:

- **50% listening and comprehension** — family speech, ThaiPod101, Netflix, songs and ChatGPT voice conversations
- **25% useful vocabulary and chunks** — especially phrases I actually hear in real life; use Drops as a regular vocabulary tool
- **25% speaking** — relaxed practice using language I already know

The goal is not to understand every word. The goal is to understand the **gist quickly enough to stay in the conversation**.

## Core tools

- **ThaiPod101 on free Spotify** — focused listening and comprehension exercises
- **Drops** — expand vocabulary and recognition in short sessions
- **ChatGPT voice** — listen, explain what I understood, fill the gaps, then respond in Thai
- **Netflix with Thai audio and English subtitles** — relaxed exposure without turning entertainment into homework
- **Thai songs** — sound recognition, vocabulary, chunks and motivation

## Learn chunks, not isolated words

Focus on whole expressions that recur in natural Thai rather than collecting vocabulary one word at a time.

For example, learn เดี๋ยว through common chunks such as:

- เดี๋ยวไป — I’ll go in a moment / later
- เดี๋ยวมา — I’ll be back / come in a moment
- เดี๋ยวก่อน — wait a moment
- เดี๋ยวค่อย… — we’ll … later / then we can …

## Repair language

Not understanding should not automatically end the Thai conversation. Practise using Thai to repair the conversation:

- เมื่อกี้พูดว่าอะไรนะครับ — What did you just say?
- พูดอีกทีได้ไหมครับ — Can you say that again?
- หมายถึงอะไรครับ — What do you mean?
- อ๋อ เข้าใจแล้ว — Ah, I get it now.

## Netflix and songs

Do not turn them into homework.

When watching Thai-dubbed Netflix with English subtitles, keep watching normally and capture only **one or two useful Thai phrases per episode**.

With songs, focus on understanding the chorus and a few reusable constructions rather than translating every poetic line.

## ChatGPT voice practice

Use natural but slightly slowed-down Thai. First say what I think I heard or understood, then check the missing 20–30%.

This should train comprehension before forcing a perfect spoken response.

## Fit Thai into normal life

Do not rely on finding a perfect study hour.

- **Train / walking:** ThaiPod101, Thai music, downloaded listening, mental narration
- **Waiting rooms / queues:** Drops, saved phrases, recall five words, silently describe what is happening
- **At home:** focused listening, ChatGPT voice, Netflix, songs, reading aloud
- **While working:** Thai music or familiar Thai audio during low-attention work; passive ThaiPod101 during mechanical tasks, but keep difficult comprehension work for moments when I can concentrate
- **Offline:** downloaded Spotify material, saved screenshots or phrases, handwriting, recall drills and thinking in Thai
- **Five spare minutes:** one small exposure is enough to keep the thread alive

## Surround myself with Thai

Thai should become part of normal life rather than a separate subject I occasionally study.

Listen regularly, use Thai before English when possible, keep visible Thai around me, and connect the language to things I already enjoy: family, food, football, boxing, films, music and travel.

When life gets busy, reduce the dose rather than stop. **Minimum viable Thai is better than putting it on the back burner for months.**

## Why keep going

Thai is useful for family relationships now and increasingly useful for my longer-term life in Thailand. It will make extended time in Thailand more independent, more social and less like living inside an English-speaking bubble.

It is also enjoyable: hearing a phrase suddenly become understandable, getting more from music and films, and solving the patterns of a very different language are satisfying in themselves.

Learning Thai gives me a demanding ongoing exercise in attention, memory, listening and pattern recognition. I do not need exaggerated brain-health claims to make that worthwhile.

## Short-term target

Understand the gist of ordinary family Thai even when I do not catch every word.

Once comprehension improves, speaking should become easier because I will have more incoming Thai to react to.
